If you have been hunting through Settings on your Apple Watch for where to set your daily step goal, you can stop. **There isn't one.**

This surprises almost everyone, because the Watch is the definitive step-tracking device and it counts your steps all day without being asked. But none of the three Activity rings is a step goal, and watchOS offers no setting to create one.

Here is what the rings actually measure, what you *can* change natively, and how to get a real step target on your wrist if that is the number you care about.

> **Quick Answer: Step Goals on Apple Watch**
> 1. **There is no native step goal.** The rings are Move (active calories), Exercise (minutes), and Stand (hours) — none of them counts steps.
> 2. **What you can change natively:** your Move goal in calories, and your Exercise and Stand targets, via the Activity app on the Watch or the Fitness app on your iPhone.
> 3. **For an actual step target,** you need a third-party app, which also gives you a step-based progress ring on the watch face.
> 4. **A good target** is a few hundred to a thousand steps above your own typical day — not a round number copied from someone else.

## Why there is no step goal on Apple Watch

Apple built the Activity rings around **effort**, not distance covered:

| Ring | What it measures | What closes it |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Move** | Active calories burned | Any activity that raises your energy expenditure |
| **Exercise** | Minutes at brisk-walk intensity or above | Sustained effort, regardless of whether you travelled anywhere |
| **Stand** | Hours in which you stood and moved for a minute | Standing up — you can earn it without going anywhere |

That is a defensible design. Calories account for intensity, which steps ignore: a hard indoor cycling session fills Move and Exercise while your step count barely moves.

But it also means the rings cannot answer the question most walkers actually have — *did I walk enough today?* Steps are the measure of that, and the rings simply are not built to show it.

There is a knock-on effect too: because there is no step goal, there is also **no native steps complication** for your watch face. Apple does not surface a number it has no target for. See [how to show steps on your Apple Watch face](https://stepswidget.app/blog/show-steps-watch-face).

## What you can change natively

Three things, all of them ring-based:

- **Move goal** — the calorie target. Fully adjustable, any time.
- **Exercise goal** — minutes per day, adjustable on watchOS 7 and later.
- **Stand goal** — hours per day, also adjustable.

What you cannot do is set a step count as a target, convert the Move ring to steps, or get the Watch to notify you about a step threshold.

## How to change your Move goal

**On the Watch:**

1. Open the **Activity** app.
2. Scroll to the bottom and tap **Change Goals**.
3. Adjust Move, then Exercise, then Stand with the Digital Crown or the + and − buttons.
4. Confirm each one.

**On your iPhone:**

1. Open the **Fitness** app.
2. Tap your profile picture, then **Change Goals**.
3. Set each target and confirm.

Worth knowing: changing the goals does not alter your history, and a goal you miss repeatedly is worse than no goal, because it trains you to ignore the ring.

## What number should you set?

The method that works is not to copy a round number but to look at your own history.

Find your typical day over the last two weeks — not your best, not your worst, the middle — and set the goal a few hundred to a thousand steps above it. Close enough that an ordinary day gets you most of the way; far enough that closing it takes a deliberate walk.

For context, the research points lower than the famous figure: a 2025 meta-analysis in *The Lancet Public Health* found the benefit curve bending sharply between 5,000 and 7,000 steps a day and flattening after, which puts a sensible health-oriented range at roughly **6,000 to 8,500**. The 10,000 figure came from a 1965 Japanese pedometer's brand name, not a study.

## Move goal or step goal — which should you use?

They answer different questions, and there is no reason to pick only one:

| Use the **Move goal** if | Use a **step goal** if |
| :--- | :--- |
| Your activity is varied — cycling, swimming, strength work | Walking is your main activity |
| You care about total energy expenditure | You care about how much you got up and moved |
| You like the ring-closing format | You want a number you can act on mid-afternoon |
| Intensity matters to you | Consistency across the whole day matters to you |

The honest division: the Move ring is better at recognising hard effort; a step goal is better at catching the slow, sedentary day where nothing was hard because nothing happened at all.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can you set a step goal on Apple Watch?** Not natively. The Activity rings track active calories, exercise minutes, and stand hours, and watchOS has no setting for a daily step target. A third-party step app provides one.

**How do I change my step goal on Apple Watch?** If you mean the Move goal: Activity app → scroll down → Change Goals, or Fitness app → profile → Change Goals on your iPhone. For an actual step target you need a third-party app, which sets its goal independently of the rings.

**Why does my Apple Watch not have a step goal?** Apple designed the rings around effort rather than distance, so calories stand in for intensity. Steps are recorded but have no target attached, which is also why there is no native steps complication.

**What is a good daily step goal?** Somewhere around 6,000 to 8,500 for most people, set a few hundred to a thousand above your own typical day. The research benefit curve flattens after roughly 7,000 to 8,000.

**Does changing my Move goal change my step count?** No. Goals only change what your activity is compared against. Your steps are recorded in Apple Health regardless.

**Can I set a step goal from my wrist rather than my phone?** With Steps Widget, yes — the Daily Goal is adjustable in the Watch app itself and syncs back to your iPhone.
