You added a step widget three weeks ago. Be honest: when did you last actually _read_ it?

Not glance past it. Read it — take in the number, compare it to your goal, decide something. For most people the answer is "about four days after installing it," and then the widget joined the wallpaper. Still there, still accurate, no longer consulted.

This is the quiet failure mode of every glanceable tool, and it has nothing to do with the data being wrong. A widget that is accurate and invisible changes your behaviour exactly as much as no widget at all.

> **Quick Answer: How to Make a Step Widget You Keep Noticing**
>
> 1. **Place it where your eye already lands** — top-left of the first Home Screen page, or the row above the dock. Prime real estate beats a bigger widget on a page you rarely open.
> 2. **Make it belong.** A widget whose colours clash with your wallpaper reads as clutter, and clutter is what your brain is best at filtering out.
> 3. **Pick the widget that answers a question.** A bare number is easy to ignore; progress against a goal, or a curve showing your momentum, asks for a decision.
> 4. **Change the look on purpose, every month or two.** Novelty is what restores attention. A seasonal restyle costs one minute and buys weeks of being seen again.
> 5. **Give it a dedicated slot.** If seeing your progress reliably is the point, do not let iOS decide what is showing when you look.

## Widget blindness is a real effect, not laziness

Your visual system is built to stop reporting things that do not change. A sound that repeats on a loop fades out of awareness within minutes; a label on a familiar machine stops being read after the first week. Attention is spent on what is new or what is moving, because everything else is already accounted for.

A Home Screen widget is an unusually strong candidate for that filtering. It sits in the same place, in the same shape, in the same colours, every single time you unlock your phone — dozens of times a day. The number inside it changes, but the _package_ does not, and the package is what your eye is sorting on. Within a couple of weeks it has been classified as furniture.

The practical consequence is specific: your step widget does not fail when it shows the wrong number. It fails when it shows the right number and you unlock straight past it into Messages.

So the design problem is not "how do I display steps." Every step widget does that. It is "how do I stay in the small set of things on this screen that still get looked at."

## The three reasons a step widget goes invisible

### 1. It does not belong

A widget in default system grey on a warm photographic wallpaper reads as an intrusion. Your brain registers it as something stuck on top of the screen rather than part of it, and the thing stuck on top is exactly what gets tuned out — the same instinct that makes people stop seeing banner ads.

A widget whose colours are drawn from the wallpaper behind it reads as part of the design. Counterintuitively, _blending in_ keeps it visible for longer than shouting, because you stop resenting it.

### 2. It is in the wrong place

Most people put a new widget wherever there happens to be a gap — often the bottom of a second page they open twice a week. A step widget on page three is a step widget you check on purpose, which means you check it when you already know the answer.

### 3. It doesn't relate to anything about you

"6,412 steps" is a fact. Facts are easy to skim past. "6,412 — 1,600 short, four hours left" is a question with a decision attached, and decisions catch attention in a way that totals do not.

## Placement: where the eye already goes

Placement does more work than any visual choice, and it is free.

| Slot                        | Good for                                                                                | Watch out for                                                                                             |
| :-------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Top-left, first page**    | The strongest slot on the phone — it is where the eye lands on unlock, in reading order | Nothing. If steps matter to you, this is the slot                                                         |
| **Row above the dock**      | Easy thumb reach, passed on every unlock                                                | Competes with the apps you open reflexively                                                               |
| **Lock Screen accessory**   | Seen before you even unlock — the lowest-effort glance there is                         | Tiny, with limited colour on iOS, so it’s great for a quick status check                                  |
| **StandBy, while charging** | Evening and desk glances, when there is still time to act                               | Only while docked — see [steps in StandBy mode](https://stepswidget.app/blog/show-steps-standby-mode-ios) |

Two further rules:

- **Give it a dedicated slot rather than burying it in a Smart Stack.** A stack with Smart Rotate on means iOS chooses what you see when you glance. Steps Widget does report a relevance score so it surfaces at useful moments — like when you have been moving recently, and when you are between 70% and just past your goal — but if reliably seeing your progress is the point, do not leave it to rotation.
- **Two widgets, two jobs.** A common setup is a ring in prime position for the constant glance, and an hourly chart somewhere secondary for when you want to know where the day went. See [the widget gallery](https://stepswidget.app/docs/widgets/widget-gallery) for what each one shows.

## Make it belong on your Home Screen

This is where customization stops being decoration and starts being function. Three levels, in increasing order of effort:

**1. Pick a palette that matches your setup.** [Steps Widget](https://stepswidget.app) offers three rows of built-in styles per widget type: solid colours including muted text tones, two-tone combinations where the number and the ring take different colours, and gradient themes — Sunrise, Meadow, Dusk, Aurora, Ocean. Each widget type keeps its own style, so a ring and a timeline on the same screen can look nothing alike.

**2. Set the appearance per widget.** Light, Dark, or System, chosen for that widget alone. A widget that stays dark on a light Home Screen is a deliberate and very effective way to make one thing stand out on a screen where everything else matches.

**3. Build a style from your own photo.** Point [Style from Photo](https://stepswidget.app/docs/widgets/photo-styles) at your wallpaper — or any picture — and it gives you two modes:

- **Colors Palette** takes colours from the picture and applies them to the normal widget design. The photo itself is not shown.
  It picks six key colours from the image, then creates several styles from them: a dark style, a dominant-colour style, a light style, a two-colour gradient, and an accent-only style that keeps the system background.

- **Background** puts the picture itself behind your step count.

Using your actual wallpaper as the source is the single fastest route to a widget that looks native to your screen, because the colours are by definition already there.

## A working recipe for a photo-background widget

Photo backgrounds go wrong in a predictable way: the picture looks great and the numbers are unreadable. The order that fixes it:

1. **Frame first.** Drag to move, pinch to zoom. The preview is the widget at real size with your real step count in it, and the rest of the photo bleeds around it dimmed, so you can see what sits just outside the frame. A freshly picked photo opens at roughly wallpaper scale, which makes the widget read as a hole cut in your Home Screen rather than as a thumbnail — usually the effect you want.
2. **Set the tone.** Left darkens and deepens, right lightens and washes out. Push it until the area _under the step count_ is clearly darker or clearly lighter than the text will be. Tone fixes more legibility problems than colour choice does.
3. **Add blur only if detail is still fighting the numbers.** Foliage, a crowd, or text behind the count usually needs a little, even when the brightness is fine.
4. **Pick the foreground colour last.** White, black, and the most vivid colour in the photo are offered, plus a full picker. The app suggests one by measuring the part of the picture the widget actually shows — but once you choose one yourself, reframing never takes it back.

Keep the busiest part of the image out from under the digits and you will get a widget that looks designed rather than improvised.

## Where a photo survives, and where it flattens

Worth knowing before you spend ten minutes framing a picture:

| Surface                      | What shows                                                                                                                                              |
| :--------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Home Screen widget**       | The full photo background, as you framed it                                                                                                             |
| **Lock Screen accessory**    | No photo — iOS draws these in a single tint on a clear background, so the style's _colours_ come through instead                                        |
| **Apple Watch complication** | Same as the Lock Screen: colours, not pictures                                                                                                          |
| **Tinted Home Screen icons** | iOS renders the widget monochrome, so the photo flattens into the tint — see [tinted widgets](https://stepswidget.app/blog/ios-18-tinted-steps-widgets) |
| **StandBy night mode**       | Also monochrome, for the same reason                                                                                                                    |

The rule of thumb: **Colors Palette is the mode that travels.** If you want one coherent look across the Lock Screen and your wrist as well as the Home Screen, build a palette style. Save the photo backgrounds for the Home Screen, where they actually render.

## Change it on purpose

Here is the part most people never think to do, and it is the one that matters most for staying noticed.

Novelty is what restores attention. A widget you restyle every month or two gets a fresh few weeks of actually being read, for about two minutes of effort. Treat it like changing your wallpaper — not a chore, a small pleasure.

Good triggers for a restyle:

- **The season turns.** A summer gradient stops feeling right in November, and the mismatch itself is a reason to look.
- **You changed your wallpaper.** The widget is now the thing that does not match. Rebuild the palette from the new picture.
- **You changed your goal.** A new target is easier to take seriously in a new colour.
- **You caught yourself ignoring it.** That is the signal, and it is the whole point of this post.

Steps Widget keeps up to 60 saved styles, and they sync across your devices through your own private iCloud, so a library of seasonal looks is a reasonable thing to build up and rotate through. You can also [share a style](https://stepswidget.app/docs/widgets/share-a-style) as a single image — one picture carries the whole style to someone else's phone.

There are seven app icons too, in **Settings › App Icon**: Classic, Black, Blue, Green, Orange, Pink, and Purple. Same instinct, applied to the icon.

## Pick the widget that asks you a question

Visual design gets you looked at. What is _in_ the widget decides whether looking does anything.

| Widget             | What it shows                              | Best when                                                 |
| :----------------- | :----------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Steps Ring**     | Current steps with goal progress in a ring | You want the constant glance — progress, not just a total |
| **Steps Timeline** | Steps by hour, as a bar or line chart      | You want to see _where the day went_                      |
| **Goal Progress**  | How close you are to today's goal          | The goal is the only thing you care about                 |
| **Steps Minimal**  | A clean step count                         | Your Home Screen is minimal and the number is enough      |
| **Steps Prompt**   | Step count with a message                  | You like a bit of encouragement with the number           |

One setting is worth singling out. On Steps Timeline you can choose **Bar Chart** or **Line Chart**:

- **Bar Chart** gives each hour its own bar — good for checking a specific hour.
- **Line Chart** plots your running total, so the line only climbs and what you read is the _slope_. A steep stretch is a walk; a flat stretch is time in the chair. That curve tracks the same trajectory the reminder model uses, so a line flattening through the afternoon is roughly the point at which a nudge becomes likely. It also suits the narrow Lock Screen and Apple Watch rectangular slots better than a row of thin bars.

If you want the widget to prompt a decision rather than report a fact, the ring and the line chart are the two that do it.

## What this costs

Worth being straightforward about, since this whole post is about appearance.

**Free forever:** goal reminders and every setting behind them, the on-device model, your daily goal, Insight, Live Activity, the Apple Watch app, Motion Sensor, the step window settings, and a working widget on every surface. Making a photo style and keeping it is free too.

**Paid:** the act of _applying_ styles, colours, and gradient themes — the Customization subscription.

Two details that matter:

- **Nothing the app needs to function is behind the paywall.** Without subscribing you get the full reminder system and your steps on every surface. It changes how it looks, not what it does.
- **A lapse does not undress your Home Screen.** The style you are already using keeps rendering, photograph and all. What an active subscription buys is switching to a _different_ one.

Styles are presentation only, so none of this touches your data. Your steps live in Apple Health, and changing a style — or letting a subscription lapse — never costs you history.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why do I stop noticing my widgets after a while?** Because your visual system filters out anything that stays the same. The widget's position, shape, and colours do not change even though the number inside does, so after a couple of weeks it gets classified as background. Changing the look periodically, and placing it where your eye already lands, is what keeps it in the set of things you still read.

**Where is the best place to put a step widget?** Top-left of your first Home Screen page, or the row above the dock. Both are passed on nearly every unlock. A small widget in a prime slot beats a large one on a page you rarely open.

**Can I make a widget match my wallpaper?** Yes. Point Style from Photo at your wallpaper and use Colors Palette — the colours are lifted from the image and applied to the widget, so it reads as part of the screen rather than something stuck on top.

**Why doesn't my photo show on the Lock Screen or Apple Watch?** iOS draws accessory widgets and complications in a single tint colour on a clear background, so there is no photo to render. Your style's colours still apply. The same happens on a tinted Home Screen and in StandBy night mode. Use a Colors Palette style for a look that travels across all surfaces.

**Does customizing my widget cost anything?** Making a photo style is free. Applying styles, colours, and gradients is the Customization subscription. Everything functional — reminders, the goal, Insight, the Watch app, the widget itself — is free, and a style already on your widget keeps rendering if the subscription lapses.

**How often should I change my widget style?** Every month or two, or whenever you catch yourself ignoring it. A restyle takes about a minute and buys weeks of the widget being genuinely seen again.
