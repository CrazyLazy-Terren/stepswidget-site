A step widget looks good on your Home Screen when it fits the screen around it: colours drawn from your wallpaper, one number you can read at arm's length, and the same type and shape as the widgets next to it. How much data it shows comes much later. Your Home Screen is the screen you see most, and people choose what goes on it the way they choose a wallpaper. A widget that clashes gets deleted, however accurate it is.

> **Quick answer: the five choices that make a step widget look right**
>
> 1. **Colour from your wallpaper.** Pick a palette that is already on the screen, so the widget reads as part of it.
> 2. **One focal number.** The step count should be the biggest, highest-contrast thing in the widget.
> 3. **Light or dark on purpose.** Match the rest of the screen, or break from it once, deliberately.
> 4. **One family.** Same font and colour logic across every widget you place, even if the layouts differ.
> 5. **The right size for the job.** Small for a number, ring or chart; medium only when you want room for a message.


The eight screens in the cover image share one wallpaper, the same five widgets and the same 8,072 steps. Only the style changes: white with navy, red, green or blue, navy with red, and charcoal with green, gold or yellow. That is the whole argument of this post: the data is identical, and which one you would keep on your phone comes down to design.

## Why does a step widget's design matter at all?

Because you will look at it hundreds of times before you ever act on it. A step count on the Home Screen is passive by design. It works by being seen in passing, dozens of times a day, without you opening anything. If you don't like how it looks, it gets moved to page three or deleted, and a widget on page three doesn't do its job.

Apple Health already counts your steps accurately. What it does not do is put that number somewhere you will see it, in a form you are happy to keep there. That second part is a design problem, and it is the part a widget is for.

There is a practical side too. A widget that matches the screen is easier to read, not just nicer to look at. Clashing colours and busy backgrounds make the digits work harder against everything around them.

## How do you pick colours that fit your wallpaper?

Start from the wallpaper, not the widget. The colours that look right are almost always ones already on the screen.

- **Take the darkest colour in the wallpaper for the background**, and the brightest or most saturated one for the number. On the pink and purple wallpaper in the cover, a charcoal background with a warm gold number looks deliberate rather than pasted on, because the gold picks up the warm pink at the top of the image.
- **Or go white and pick one accent.** White widgets on a colourful wallpaper read like the system's own widgets. One accent colour for the progress bar, ring or chart keeps them from looking blank.
- **Limit yourself to two colours per widget**, plus white or black. A third colour is usually where things start to look busy.

Steps Widget can do the picking for you. [Style from Photo](https://stepswidget.app/docs/widgets/photo-styles) reads six key colours from any picture, including your wallpaper, and builds a set of styles from them: dark, light, dominant-colour, a two-colour gradient and an accent-only style. Because the colours come from the wallpaper itself, they match by definition.

If you use iOS Tinted mode, the system recolours every icon and widget for you. See [iOS 18 tinted widgets](https://stepswidget.app/blog/ios-18-tinted-steps-widgets) for how a step widget behaves there.

## What should be the focal point of a step widget?

The step count. It should be the largest element and the one with the most contrast, so you can read it from arm's length in under a second.

Everything else is supporting detail and should look like it: the time of the last update smaller and lighter, the icon small, the progress bar or ring thinner than the digits are tall. When a widget looks cluttered, it is usually because two or three elements are competing at the same size.

A quick test: hold your phone at arm's length and squint. If the number is the first thing you can make out, the hierarchy is right.

Fonts matter more here than people expect. Steps Widget offers three:

| Font            | Feels like                                    | Suits                                         |
| :-------------- | :-------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------- |
| **Rounded**     | The system's own rounded font (the default)   | Almost any setup; it matches Apple's widgets  |
| **Handwritten** | A marker-pen note on the fridge               | Warm, playful or photo-heavy Home Screens     |
| **Digital**     | A narrow scoreboard or road-sign face         | Minimal, dark or monochrome setups            |

The font belongs to the style, so every widget using that style changes together.

## Should your widget be light or dark?

Either can look good. What matters is that it is a choice, not a default.

- **Match the screen** when you want a calm, uniform layout. On a light Home Screen, light widgets; on a dark wallpaper, dark ones.
- **Break from it once** when you want the step count to stand out. A dark widget draws the eye on a bright wallpaper. In the cover, the charcoal and navy screens are the ones you notice first, even though they show exactly the same numbers as the white ones.

In Steps Widget, appearance (System, Light or Dark) is set per widget, so you can keep everything else on System and pin one widget to Dark.

## How do you make several widgets look like one set?

Pick one rule and apply it to every widget you place. The layouts can differ (a ring, a chart, a message), as long as the font and colour logic stay the same.

## Which widget size looks best?

The smallest one that shows what you need.

| Size       | Holds comfortably                                           | Looks best as                                    |
| :--------- | :---------------------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------- |
| **Small**  | One number plus one supporting element: a ring, bar or chart | The everyday glance; pairs neatly with app icons |
| **Medium** | A number with a short message, or your steps beside a friend's | The one "feature" widget on a page            |

Every Steps Widget layout comes in small, and Steps Prompt, Goal Progress and Steps Minimal also come in medium. A grid of small widgets looks tidy because they line up with the app icons. One medium widget among them gives the page a focal point. Two medium widgets stacked usually looks heavy.

For where on the screen to put it, see [why you stopped noticing your step widget](https://stepswidget.app/blog/step-widget-you-actually-notice), which covers placement and keeping the widget seen over time.

## Four Home Screen looks to start from

**Minimal.** White or system background, black number, one muted accent, Rounded font, small size. Matches Apple's own widgets and works on any wallpaper.

**Matched.** Run your wallpaper through Style from Photo, pick the dark or dominant-colour style, and use it on every widget. The fastest way to a screen that looks designed.

**Photo.** Put the picture itself behind the step count using Style from Photo's background mode. Darken the tone until the digits read clearly, and add blur only if the detail still fights the number. This one is Home Screen only, since iOS draws Lock Screen and Apple Watch widgets in a single tint.

**Statement.** One dark widget, a gradient theme like Dusk or Ocean, or the Digital font, on an otherwise calm screen. Use it when you want the step count to be the first thing you see.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I make my step widget match my wallpaper?** Use colours that are already in the wallpaper: the darkest for the background and the brightest for the number. In Steps Widget, Style from Photo reads the colours from your wallpaper and builds matching styles for you.

**Should a step widget be light or dark?** Match the rest of your Home Screen for a calm look. Use a single dark widget on a light screen, or the other way round, when you want the step count to stand out.

**What size step widget looks best?** Small, for most people. It lines up with the app icons and fits one number with a ring or bar. Use medium when you want room for a message, and keep it to one per page.

**Can I change the font on a step widget?** Yes. Steps Widget offers Rounded, Handwritten and Digital. The font is part of the style, so every widget using that style changes together.

**Why doesn't my photo widget show on the Lock Screen?** iOS draws Lock Screen widgets and Apple Watch complications in a single tint on a clear background, so photos can't render there. Your style's colours still apply. Use a palette style if you want one look across every surface.

**Does styling a widget change my step data?** No. Styles are presentation only. Your steps stay in Apple Health, and switching styles never touches your history.
