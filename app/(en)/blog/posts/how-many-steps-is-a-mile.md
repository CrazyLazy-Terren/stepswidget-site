**About 2,100 steps for an average adult.** That is the short answer, and for most purposes it is good enough.

The longer answer is more useful, because the number moves by several hundred depending on who is walking and how fast. A tall person walking briskly might cover a mile in under 2,000 steps; a shorter person strolling might need 2,600. Both are normal.

Here is the conversion by height and by pace, what the common step targets work out to in distance, and the one conversion that is more practical than any of them.

> **Quick Answer: Steps per Mile and Kilometre**
> - **1 mile ≈ 2,000–2,500 steps** for most adults walking. Call it **2,100** if you want one number.
> - **1 kilometre ≈ 1,250–1,550 steps.** Call it **1,300**.
> - **10,000 steps ≈ 4.5 miles / 7.5 km** at an average stride.
> - **Running covers more ground per step** — roughly 1,400–1,700 steps per mile, because your stride lengthens.
> - **Height is the biggest variable.** Taller legs, longer steps, fewer steps per mile.

## The simple answer

A mile is 5,280 feet, or 1,609 metres. Divide that by your step length and you have your answer.

An average adult walking step is roughly **2.2 to 2.5 feet** (about 67–76 cm), which puts a mile at **2,100 to 2,400 steps**. The commonly quoted range of 2,000–2,500 covers nearly everyone walking at an ordinary pace.

For a kilometre, divide by about 1.61: roughly **1,300 to 1,500 steps**.

A note on terminology, because the two get mixed up: a **step** is one foot striking the ground. A **stride** is two steps — one full cycle of both legs. Everything on this page uses steps, which is what your phone counts.

## Steps per mile by height

Height is the strongest predictor of step length. A reasonable estimate for walking is that your step length is around **41–42% of your height**, which gives:

| Height | Approx. step length | Steps per mile | Steps per km |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 5'0" (152 cm) | 63 cm | ~2,560 | ~1,590 |
| 5'4" (163 cm) | 67 cm | ~2,390 | ~1,490 |
| 5'6" (168 cm) | 69 cm | ~2,320 | ~1,440 |
| 5'8" (173 cm) | 71 cm | ~2,250 | ~1,400 |
| 5'10" (178 cm) | 73 cm | ~2,190 | ~1,360 |
| 6'0" (183 cm) | 76 cm | ~2,130 | ~1,320 |
| 6'2" (188 cm) | 78 cm | ~2,070 | ~1,290 |
| 6'4" (193 cm) | 80 cm | ~2,010 | ~1,250 |

The gap between the top and bottom of that table is more than 500 steps per mile — about 25%. If you are comparing step counts with someone of a very different height, you are not comparing the same distance.

## How pace changes the number

Your stride lengthens as you speed up, so faster walking means fewer steps for the same distance:

| Pace | Typical steps per mile | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Slow stroll** (~2 mph) | 2,400–2,700 | Short, unhurried steps |
| **Average walk** (~3 mph) | 2,100–2,400 | The default assumption |
| **Brisk walk** (~4 mph) | 1,900–2,100 | Longer, more purposeful steps |
| **Jogging** (~5–6 mph) | 1,500–1,700 | Stride opens up considerably |
| **Running** (7+ mph) | 1,300–1,500 | Longest stride, fewest steps |

This is why a run and a walk of the same distance produce noticeably different step counts, and why a day with a run in it can look lighter on a step counter than it felt.

## What the common step targets work out to

| Steps | Miles | Kilometres | Walking time at ~100 steps/min |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1,000 | ~0.5 | ~0.8 | ~10 minutes |
| 2,500 | ~1.2 | ~1.9 | ~25 minutes |
| 5,000 | ~2.4 | ~3.8 | ~50 minutes |
| 7,000 | ~3.3 | ~5.4 | ~70 minutes |
| 8,000 | ~3.8 | ~6.1 | ~80 minutes |
| 10,000 | ~4.7 | ~7.6 | ~100 minutes |

And in reverse, for the distances people actually walk:

- **A 5K** is roughly **6,500 steps**.
- **A 10K** is roughly **13,000 steps**.
- **A half marathon** walked is roughly **27,000 steps**.

Note that these are *total* walking time, not extra time — most of those steps happen during a normal day rather than in one block.

## How to measure your own stride

Two minutes of effort gives you a figure more accurate than any table:

1. Find a measured distance — a 400 m running track is ideal, since one lap is a quarter mile plus a few metres.
2. Walk it at your normal pace, counting your steps or watching your phone's count before and after.
3. Divide: steps ÷ distance. On a 400 m track, multiply your lap count by 4.02 for steps per mile, or by 2.5 for steps per km.

Do it twice and average. If you want separate figures for walking and running, measure both — they will differ by several hundred steps per mile.

## Why your phone's distance may not match

Your iPhone and Apple Watch both report walking distance, and it will not always agree with a conversion you do by hand.

- **They estimate stride from your health details.** Height, weight, age, and sex all feed the estimate. If those are wrong or missing in **Health › profile › Health Details**, the distance will be off even when the step count is right.
- **The Watch calibrates with GPS.** A 20-minute outdoor walk workout in an open area lets watchOS learn your actual stride at various paces, which improves distance estimates considerably.
- **Treadmill distance is different again.** A treadmill measures belt movement; your phone measures your motion. Neither is wrong — see [do walking pad steps count](https://stepswidget.app/blog/do-walking-pad-steps-count).

Steps are counted directly. Distance is always an estimate built on top of them, which is why steps are the more reliable number to track day to day.

## The conversion that is actually more useful

Here is the practical point, and it is not about distance at all.

Knowing a mile is 2,100 steps is trivia unless you are planning a route. The conversion that changes behaviour is **steps into minutes**, because minutes are something you can put in your day:

> At an ordinary walking cadence of roughly **100 steps per minute**, 1,000 steps is about **10 minutes**.

So a 2,000-step gap at 4 PM is not an abstract shortfall — it is a 20-minute walk before dinner. That reframing is the whole reason to know your own numbers, and it works whether you think in miles, kilometres, or neither.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many steps is a mile?** Roughly 2,000 to 2,500 for most adults walking, averaging about 2,100. Taller people take fewer steps per mile; a faster pace also lowers the count.

**How many steps is a kilometre?** Roughly 1,250 to 1,550, averaging about 1,300.

**How many miles is 10,000 steps?** About 4.7 miles, or 7.6 km, at an average walking stride — roughly 100 minutes of walking spread across a day.

**How many steps is a 5K?** About 6,500 walking. Running a 5K takes fewer, usually 4,500–5,500, because your stride lengthens.

**Does running take fewer steps per mile than walking?** Yes. Running stride is considerably longer, so a mile run is roughly 1,300–1,700 steps against 2,100–2,400 walking.

**How do I work out my own steps per mile?** Walk a measured distance such as a 400 m track lap at your normal pace and count the steps, then scale up. Multiply a lap count by about 4.02 for steps per mile.

**Why does my phone's distance not match my step count?** Distance is estimated from your stride, which iOS infers from your health details, while steps are counted directly. Correcting your height and weight in Health, and doing a calibration walk with an Apple Watch, both improve it.
