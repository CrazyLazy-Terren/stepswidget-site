If you use a wheelchair, steps are the wrong number. **Wheelchair Mode** switches the whole app to the **pushes** your Apple Watch records instead, so your widgets, your goal, and your reminders all measure the movement you actually make.

## What you need

An **Apple Watch** with Apple's own wheelchair setting turned on. The iPhone does not count pushes; the Watch does, and only once it knows you use a wheelchair:

1. Open the **Health** app on your iPhone.
2. Tap your profile picture, then **Health Details**.
3. Tap **Edit** and set **Wheelchair** to **Yes**.

From then on, the Watch records pushes into Apple Health, and Steps reads them from there.

## Turning it on

Either place works:

- **On the first screen**, when you open the app for the first time, before you connect Apple Health.
- **In Settings**, under **Daily Goal**: turn on **Wheelchair Mode** — *"Count pushes from Apple Watch instead of steps."*

The first time it goes on, the app asks Apple Health for read access to **wheelchair push count**. Allow it, or the count stays at zero.

The setting follows to your Apple Watch on its own.

## What changes

Every number the app reads switches from steps to pushes at once:

- **Widgets and the Live Activity** show your pushes, and the accessory widgets on the Lock Screen and Apple Watch use a wheelchair figure instead of a walker.
- **Your daily goal** is counted in pushes. Set a figure that fits a push day — it is not the same scale as steps.
- **Goal reminders** project your pushes for the rest of the day, and the built-in messages talk about pushes and rolling: **Falling behind** arrives as *"Let's roll"*. Messages you wrote yourself are sent as you wrote them. See [Reminder messages](/docs/goal-reminders/reminder-messages).
- **The main screen** reads *"Likely to reach 2,400 pushes today"* rather than steps.
- **Step Sources** lists where your pushes came from.

Your step history is untouched. Turn the mode off and everything goes back to steps.

## What is not available

**Motion Sensor** stays off. It reads the iPhone's pedometer, which counts steps, not pushes, so it would add the wrong thing. Your count updates as Apple Health receives pushes from the Watch. See [Motion Sensor](/docs/steps-and-data/motion-sensor-and-live-activity).

**Walking Plan** is hidden from Settings. Its targets and calorie maths are built for walking. A plan you started before turning the mode on keeps running. See [Walking Plan](/docs/getting-started/walking-plan#does-it-work-with-wheelchair-mode).

## Why does my count stay at zero?

Work through these in order:

1. **Is the Watch set to wheelchair?** Without Apple's setting in Health Details, the Watch counts steps and records no pushes at all.
2. **Does Health have pushes?** Open **Health › Browse › Activity › Wheelchair Pushes**. If it is empty, the Watch has not recorded any yet.
3. **Can Steps read them?** **Health** › your profile picture › **Apps and Services** › **Steps Widget**, and turn on **Wheelchair Pushes**.
